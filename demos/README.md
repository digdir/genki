# Demos

Each file here is a narrated demo of genki: a title, a voice, a start page and steps. A step is one line the voice reads and the clicks shown while it is read. `scripts/demo.ts` turns a file into an mp4 with voice and subtitles in `demos/out/<name>/`.

```yaml
title: En rask tur i GenKI-portalen
language: nb-NO
voice: nb-NO-PernilleNeural
viewport: [1920, 1080]
start: https://portal.genki-test.digdir.cloud
steps:
  - say: Fellestjenestene er klare fra første dag.
    do:
      - [find, role, link, click, --name, Fellestjenester]
    hold: 1
```

`do` takes [agent-browser](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-browser) commands: `find`, `click`, `fill`, `type`, `press`, `hover`, `select`, `scroll`, `wait`, `open` and a few more. Commands that read the page's storage or run code, such as `eval`, `storage` and `cookies`, are refused, and so is any flag but `--name`, `--exact`, `--text`, `--timeout`, `--load` and `--url`. A click or fill is tried again for up to 15 seconds while the screen loads; after that the recording stops with the step, its line and the command, and leaves `failed-step-<n>.png` (`failed-step-0.png` when the start page never shows `ready`). A browser command that hangs is stopped after three minutes. `hold` is the seconds to wait after the line has been read (0.6 by default). The voice starts once the last command that changes the page (a click, `open`, `wait` and the like) has finished and the page has settled; a `hover` after it runs while the line is read. Before a click, hover or fill, the pointer glides to the element, scrolling smoothly when it is off screen, and pauses briefly, so it never jumps. Without `viewport` a demo is recorded at 1920 x 1080.

A demo is cut like a launch film. `intro` and `outro` add a title card before it and a closing card after it (`title`, and optionally `subtitle` and `presenter`), with the Digdir emblem. `chapter` on a step starts a chapter, shown as a title at the top of the film. `focus` on a step hints at what to zoom to while the line is read: `[role, table]`, `[role, heading, Statistikk]`, `[text, Hvor data behandles]`, `[label, Formål]` or `[css, main table]`; a focus the recorder cannot find is left out. How a film is made from this is under [How a film is made](#how-a-film-is-made).

```yaml
intro:
  title: Genki
  subtitle: Generativ KI for offentlige virksomheter
  presenter: Ola Nordmann, Digdir
steps:
  - say: Katalogen viser hvor dataene behandles.
    chapter: En rask tur
    do:
      - [find, role, link, click, --name, Katalog]
    focus: [text, Hvor data behandles]
```

`signedIn: true` signs the portal and console in with the key in `DEMO_API_KEY`, the way the smoke test does: agent-browser answers each app's `/auth/me` as signed in to the demo tenant (the console as an operator) and sends the key as a bearer token only to the apps' own origins, never to the login issuer or anyone else. Without the key that demo is skipped. `ready` is a text on the start page to wait for before recording starts.

## Voice, sound and example data

`voice: none` («Uten stemme») records with captions only. A voice that reads a name wrongly is helped by a respelling, which changes only what the voice is sent, never the captions: the platform keeps a glossary per language and provider in `demoRespellings` in `packages/contracts/src/demo-voices.ts` (ElevenLabs says «Genn-ki» and «Digg-dir»), a demo adds its own under `respell`, and a step can give a whole line in `spoken`. The step's own `spoken` wins over the demo's glossary, which wins over the platform's.

```yaml
voice: elevenlabs-film
respell:
  elevenlabs:
    Ansattporten: Ansatt-porten
music: pulse
exampleData: true
steps:
  - say: Skal dere lage en KI-tjeneste? Kom i gang på genki.digdir.cloud.
    spoken:
      elevenlabs: Skal dere lage en KI-tjeneste? Kom i gang på Genn-ki punktum Digg-dir punktum cloud.
```

`music: pulse` lays a music bed under the film, generated by the recorder so it carries no third-party rights. It is ducked about 9 dB while the voice speaks, and the voice is mixed to about -15 LUFS and the music to about -37 LUFS (-23 LUFS without a voice), measured with ffmpeg's `ebur128`; the measured loudness is kept on the recording as `sound`.

`exampleData: true` records against a genki running on example data instead of genki-test: the portal and console in mock mode (`VITE_API=mock`), at `http://portal.localhost:5173` and `http://console.localhost:5174` unless `DEMO_EXAMPLE_PORTAL_URL` and `DEMO_EXAMPLE_CONSOLE_URL` say otherwise. The start page and every `open` are moved to those apps, a signed-in demo presses «Logg inn» in the mock login instead of using `DEMO_API_KEY`, and the film carries an «Eksempeldata» tag. On every capture, retired and internal words (`demoScrubbedWords`, and the demo's own `scrub`) are hidden from the page before it is filmed.

A value that looks like a key or token is refused in a demo, by the recorder and by the API; keys live in Key Vault.

## Scenes

A step with `scene` is filmed like the walkthrough film instead of as a screen recording. A scene lasts until a step names another scene, so the lines that follow share it; after a chapter card it starts again. The templates, with the names the film's scenes have in Norwegian:

- **Tittel** (`title`): a statement on the dark brand background. `title`, `subtitle`, a `centre` box with up to six `items` around it, or the Genki logo (`logo: true`) with a row of `items` over a `base` bar.
- **Skjerm** (`screen`): the app in a light browser frame on the light brand background, with the scene's `title` above it and the chapter as the section label («DEL 1 · ...»). An optional `question` is shown large on its own during the scene's first line, and the frame comes in with the next; a screen scene of one line shows only the question.
- **Kapittelkort**: `chapter` on a step. With scenes, it is a full-screen card of 2.6 seconds before the step, listing every chapter of the film with this one lit; without scenes, it is a title over the app.
- **Tavle** (`board`): a `title`, such as «Hvor langt vi har kommet», over one to three `columns`, each with a `title`, a `mark` (`done`, `now` or `next`) and its `items`.
- **Kode** (`code`): a short excerpt of an example definition in `packages/definitions/examples`, named by its id in `definition`, beside an `eyebrow`, a `title` and a `text`. A soft hyphen (`\u00ad`) in the title marks where a long word may break.
- **Avslutning** (`close`): the logo, a `question`, the `action` and the `address` in a pill, and the Digdir line.

An item is a text, or `{ text, cue }`: it appears when the voice says the cue word, in order through the scene's lines. A demo with scenes is filmed in the film look: no pointer, no clicks shown and the camera moves inside the frame, so the way to a screen is cut and each line shows its shot. `page` on a step opens a path on the demo's app, such as `/#/developer/frontends`, before the line, and `focus` picks the region to zoom to inside the frame. `colorScheme: light` records the app in its light colours, as the film shows it (dark by default), and a viewport of `[1440, 674]` fills the frame exactly; another shape is fitted inside it. Colours come from the Digdir theme the portal and console load, and the logo and mascot from `docs/images`.

```yaml
colorScheme: light
viewport: [1440, 674]
steps:
  - say: Og hvor møter folk tjenesten?
    scene:
      template: screen
      title: Én tjeneste, mange innganger
      question: Hvor møter folk tjenesten?
    page: /#/developer/frontends
  - say: I en egen app på egen adresse, der ansatte logger inn med Ansattporten.
```

## How a film is made

The recorder captures, it does not film. It records the app at one and a half times the pixels without a pointer and in the dark colour scheme unless the demo asks for `colorScheme: light`, so the portal and the console look the same, and writes `events.json` next to it: for each step when it started, when it left the page, when the page was ready and the voice started, where the pointer moved and clicked, the boxes of the page's headings, tables and sections, and the spoken words with their times (Azure Speech fast transcription of each clip, kept as `<clip>.words.json` in `demos/out/.voice`).

The film in [genki-walkthrough.yaml](genki-walkthrough.yaml) is the four-minute Genki walkthrough as a demo: its lines are steps with scenes, its two parts are chapters, and it runs on example data in the light colour scheme with music.

An AI director then writes the edit decision list, `edl.json`: the shots (page loading is always cut out, page changes pass through the window's dark background, so two pages never show at once), camera moves tied to the word they serve, callouts, chapters, cards and the pace (`lead` and `tail` around each line). It sees the script with the word timings, the event log and a keyframe of every step. Its edit is checked against a rubric, by rules in `scripts/demo-edl.ts` and by the model looking at proof frames rendered around every camera move and cut; it revises until the review passes or three rounds have gone. Each round's edit and critique are kept as `edl-round-<n>.json` and `review-round-<n>.json`. The director runs when `DEMO_AUDIO_ENDPOINT` is set and someone has approved a cost cap for the recording (it uses `DEMO_DIRECTOR_MODEL`, `gpt-5.5` by default, on the same resource); it estimates its cost before it starts and makes a plain edit instead when that is more than what is left of the cap. Without it, a plain edit is made from the `focus` and `chapter` hints, and `DEMO_DIRECTOR=off` asks for that.

The renderer makes the film from the capture and the edit list alone, so the same `edl.json` always gives the same film: the app in a floating window that fills almost the whole width, on a dark Digdir backdrop, a spring camera with motion blur, a drawn pointer that glides, ripples on a click and hides when idle, a slight push in on clicks, captions, chapter titles and cards in Inter. It renders 60 frames a second at 1920 x 1080 to H.264. To render again from a kept capture, for example after editing `edl.json` by hand:

```sh
node scripts/demo.ts film demos/out/<demo>
```

In the console, Demoer has a studio for a recorded film: the edit list as a timeline, the film beside it, and a box for an instruction to the director, such as "hold longer on the model table". The instruction is queued like a recording, with a reason and an approved cost, and the recorder revises `edl.json` and renders the film again from the same capture and the same voice clips, which the first recording keeps as `voice-<hash>.mp3` with their word timings.

## What a film may cost

The recorder makes a priced call (a voice read by a model or ElevenLabs and each take heard back, the hear-back of an Azure Speech line, word timings, the director and an edit) only for a recording someone has approved a cost cap for. In the console the person who starts a recording sees what it is expected to cost, at most 25 USD, and ticks to approve it; a demo with a model voice, and every edit, cannot be queued without that. The recorder checks the worst case of each line, word timing and director call against what is left of the cap before it makes it, stops when the cap would be passed, and reports what it spent on the recording, where the console shows it. Without approval an Azure Speech voice still reads the lines, a model voice is left out unless its clips are cached, words are timed by estimate and the cut is plain. A local run approves with `DEMO_COST_CAP_USD`.

## Record one

You need `agent-browser`, `ffmpeg` and the Azure CLI, logged in with `az login` to a subscription that has an Azure AI Speech resource (an AI Services resource works). The voice is called with your Entra token; no key is stored.

```sh
export SPEECH_RESOURCE=<resource name> SPEECH_RESOURCE_GROUP=<resource group> SPEECH_SUBSCRIPTION=<subscription id>
node scripts/demo.ts demos/portal-tour.yaml
node scripts/demo.ts check                 # validate every demo and estimate its length
node scripts/demo.ts record --only playground
node scripts/demo.ts record --silent demos/portal-tour.yaml   # subtitles only, no voice
```

Voices come from providers, listed in `packages/contracts/src/demo-voices.ts` and in the console: Azure Speech's neural voices, the model voice `cedar` (Azure OpenAI `gpt-audio-1.5`), and ElevenLabs (`elevenlabs-film`, the voice of the walkthrough, on `eleven_v4`).

A voice read by a model, `cedar` or ElevenLabs, reads each line up to four times. Each take is heard back with Azure Speech and scored word by word against the shown text: two points for each content word it did not hear, one more if that word is a key term such as «Genki», «Ansattporten» or «MCP» (`demoKeyTerms`), one for each word of four letters or more heard that was never written, and two when a line of eight words or more is read slower than 1.6 or faster than 3.2 words a second. A take that fails after an earlier one was heard keeps the earlier one. A take that scores 0 is kept at once; otherwise the best of the four is kept. With an approved cap, an Azure Speech line is heard back and scored once too. The score of each line is kept beside its clip in `demos/out/.voice/<clip>.json` and on the recording as `lines`, where the console shows the lines that were heard with differences.

`voice: cedar` reads the lines with the model voice the lab's Friday Win videos use: `gpt-audio-1.5` on Azure OpenAI, told to read word for word in natural eastern Norwegian bokmål. It also needs `ffmpeg`, an approved cap (`DEMO_COST_CAP_USD` for a local run) and `DEMO_AUDIO_ENDPOINT`, the https endpoint of the resource with the `gpt-audio-1.5` deployment, which your Entra login must be allowed to call (Cognitive Services OpenAI User):

```sh
export DEMO_AUDIO_ENDPOINT=https://<resource>.cognitiveservices.azure.com
```

A line costs well under one krone; the voice run prints the tokens it used and what it spent of the approved cap.

ElevenLabs needs an approved cap and `DEMO_ELEVENLABS_KEY_SECRET`, the URL of the Key Vault secret that holds the ElevenLabs key (`https://<vault>.vault.azure.net/secrets/<name>`). The recorder reads the key with its own identity (the managed identity, or your `az login` locally, which needs Key Vault Secrets User on the secret), keeps it in memory for the run and sends it only to ElevenLabs. It never reads a key from the environment, a demo or this repository. A character costs 0.0003 USD in the estimate, and a film stops before it has voiced 7,500 characters.

For timing only, `DEMO_PLACEHOLDER_VOICE=Nora` reads every line with that macOS voice instead, without a model, a key or a cost, and keeps those clips apart from the real voice's:

```sh
DEMO_PLACEHOLDER_VOICE=Nora node scripts/demo.ts record --only genki-walkthrough
```

Without the speech settings, and without a saved clip for every line, a demo is recorded with subtitles only.

To fill the console's library, also set `DEMO_API_URL` and `DEMO_RECORDER_KEY`: the recording is then started, followed step by step and finished in the API. `node scripts/demo.ts recording <id>` records a recording queued from the console. In genki-test the `demo-recorder` workflow does that every five minutes: `node scripts/demo.ts queued` lists the queue to `demos/out/queued.json`, `voice --queued` voices its lines and `recording --queued` records each in turn. With `GENAI_DEMO_RECORDER=job` the API instead starts the job `genai-test-demo-recorder`, which runs the same three steps in the `genai-recorder` image and signs in with its own identity: set `DEMO_RECORDER_AUDIENCE` instead of `DEMO_RECORDER_KEY`, and `DEMO_API_KEY_SECRET` to the Key Vault secret with the sign-in key instead of `DEMO_API_KEY` ([docs/runbook.md](../docs/runbook.md#demo-recorder-job)).

Each line is sent to the voice once and cached in `demos/out/.voice`, so recording again only pays for changed lines. A recording drives genki-test like a person would, so record against the test environment only. Of the demos, only `playground` asks genki's models a question; the recorder's own model calls are under [What a film may cost](#what-a-film-may-cost).

See `docs/design/demos/brief.md` for where this is going.
